MEMORY LOOM / CONCEPT ONLY

Your marks.
Not a map of your mind.

Draw something, keep it, then rub or scatter part of it. Recall compares the fragment against up to three marks you saved.

What is actually computed

This mock uses masked nearest-pattern matching. It compares painted cells with stored patterns only where the fabric is still observed; erased cells are unknown. The closest sufficiently matching mark is restored. A nearly tied match stays unresolved. It cannot recover a mark that was never kept.

This is template retrieval, not a Hopfield network, learned synapses, or a model of your memory. The luminous cloth and flowing reconstruction are original artistic encodings. They are not growing brain tissue.

What the concept could become

A future associative-memory simulation could make interference, capacity and partial-cue recall part of the play. It would need its own model and validation. This mock tests whether making and recovering a personal mark is compelling first.

Your input stays here

No uploads, external requests, accounts or persistence. Reloading clears everything. Keyboard users can focus the fabric, move with arrows, draw with Space, or rub with Delete. The loop button offers an optional sample. Reduced-motion preferences stop the fabric drift and make recall immediate.
